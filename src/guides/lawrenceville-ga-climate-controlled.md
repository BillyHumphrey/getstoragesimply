## When climate control matters in Georgia

Lawrenceville sits in a humid subtropical climate. The nearest long-running official weather station is Atlanta Hartsfield-Jackson International Airport (NOAA station USW00013874), roughly 30 miles southwest. Local conditions in Gwinnett can differ, but the official normals are a solid reference for how hot and sticky a north Georgia summer usually is.

According to NOAA’s 1991–2020 monthly normals for that station, the mean daily maximum in July is 90.1°F, with a mean daily minimum of 71.8°F. August is close behind, at 89.0°F and 71.3°F. Those are air temperatures in a standard instrument shelter. The interior of a metal, drive-up storage unit can run hotter.

Humidity is the other half of the story. The 1991–2020 monthly normals file we queried from NOAA did not include relative humidity. The commonly cited Atlanta climate table, drawing on NOAA’s 1961–1990 normals, reports average relative humidity of 69.8% in June, 74.4% in July, and 74.8% in August. Older normals are not a perfect picture of today, but they are official measurements, and they match what most people already feel here in midsummer: the air holds a lot of moisture.

That combination is hard on some belongings and mostly irrelevant to others.

Climate control is usually worth asking about if you are storing wood furniture, veneer, musical instruments, consumer electronics, photographs, books, important papers, artwork, a mattress, leather, or clothing for more than a short stretch of mild weather. Wood swells and shrinks as humidity changes. Paper and fabric can mildew. Electronics do not love repeated heat and condensation.

It may be less important if you are storing tools, plastic bins, or yard equipment that already lives in a garage, or if you only need a unit for a few cooler weeks. A car, boat, or RV is typically parked in a different kind of space altogether; climate-controlled interior units are rarely the right product for a vehicle.

“Climate-controlled” is also not a single standard. Space Shop describes typical winter and summer set points of about 60°F and 80°F. CubeSmart describes a 55°F to 80°F range as a reasonable effort. Extra Space’s public materials describe a typical 55°F to 80°F range. U-Haul’s two Lawrenceville location pages say 65°F to 85°F, depending on the system, and only the Pleasant Hill store also lists humidity control as a distinct amenity. If the distinction matters to you, ask what is measured, what the range is, and whether any of it is written into the lease.

## Questions to ask before you sign

Introductory rates are common in this business. A reservation may lock today’s advertised price for move-in without locking that price for the life of the rental. Before you sign, these questions are worth asking out loud, and worth getting in writing if the answer matters to your budget.

1. **Is the price I am being quoted a promotional or “web” rate?** If so, what is the ongoing rate once the promotion ends, and on what date does it end?
2. **How soon can the rate increase after I move in?** Some operators can raise month-to-month rent after a 30-day notice. Others advertise a lock on base rent for a set period.
3. **If there is a rate lock, what does it cover?** Ask whether it applies only to base rent, or also to insurance, administrative fees, and late fees. U-Haul’s published 1-Year Price Lock, for example, applies to base rent and excludes optional SafeStor coverage and late fees.
4. **If I prepay several months, does that lock the rate?** Extra Space’s public FAQ describes prepaying up to six months as a way to lock a rate for the prepaid period. That is not the same as a year-long lock, and a company can change how prepay works. Confirm it for your unit.
5. **How will I be notified of an increase?** Ask whether notice comes by email, mail, or both, and make sure the facility has a current address you will actually see.
6. **What temperature and humidity range does “climate-controlled” mean at this building?** Ask whether the range is in the rental agreement or only in marketing, and whether humidity is controlled or only temperature.
7. **Is the climate-controlled unit interior-only?** If you need ground-floor or elevator access, say so before you reserve.
8. **Are there admin fees, lock fees, or a required protection plan?** Several Lawrenceville operators charge or require at least one of these. U-Haul’s public policy is that it does not charge an admin fee or deposit and does not require its insurance.
9. **If I need parking for a car, boat, or RV, is it available at this address?** Brand-wide marketing is not a guarantee that this location has a space that fits your vehicle.
10. **What notice do I have to give when I move out?** Month-to-month still usually comes with a notice rule.

## Operator policies, in brief

**Public Storage.** Month-to-month. The company says rates may change and that customers receive at least 30 days’ notice by email or mail. We found no advertised post-move-in rate lock. A reservation, according to the company’s terms, guarantees the advertised price, not the availability of a particular unit.

**U-Haul.** The two Lawrenceville stores listed above advertise U-Haul’s 1-Year Price Lock on base monthly rent at owned-and-managed locations, with no admin fee or deposit. After twelve months, the company says it may raise rent with 30 days’ notice by email and letter. The lock is not described as covering U-Box containers.

**CubeSmart.** Month-to-month. The company FAQ says prices can change daily, that a no-commitment reservation guarantees the website price, and that rate-change notices go out 30 days ahead by mail or certified email. We found no advertised post-move-in rate lock.

**Extra Space Storage.** Month-to-month. The company’s public FAQ says the original rate is not guaranteed for the life of the rental, that prepaying up to six months is offered, and that increases often come during summer peak season. Because we could not load the live FAQ page, treat that wording as something to confirm.

**Space Shop Self Storage.** Month-to-month at the Lawrenceville location. No public rate-lock or notice-period statement found.

**Mini Storage Depot.** Month-to-month. The Lawrenceville page is explicit that introductory promotional pricing is limited, can change, and does not cover protection plans or administrative fees.
